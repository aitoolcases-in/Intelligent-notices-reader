# Accrevise video flow: character and storytelling

Every Accrevise video uses this flow. It comes from the reference reel "Personal loans, explained" (88 s, 13 beats).
Visual guide: https://claude.ai/artifact/HUxSZxUgjKEeMTmHwpUQ8V

## The 13 beats

| # | Time | Beat | Reference scene | What it does |
|---|------|------|-----------------|--------------|
| 1 | 0–5 s | Hook + promise | "Personal loans, *explained.*" over a sketch of the hero and the car, series tag "in 90 seconds", key numbers ($20,000 · 10 % · 5 years) | Topic, series and the example's numbers up front |
| 2 | 5–12 s | Meet the hero | "Leo needs a *car*": car with a $20,000 tag, "his savings: $0 → a personal loan" | A name, a need and a gap. The problem leads to the product |
| 3 | 12–20 s | Define it | "A personal *loan*": a blank agreement, then ticks one by one (fixed amount, usually no collateral, equal monthly payments) | Three plain features, revealed one at a time |
| 4 | 20–26 s | The offer | "Leo's *offer*": amount, rate and term boxes pop in, with a footnote | Fix the numbers used for the rest of the video |
| 5 | 26–35 s | The real cost | "60 payments *later*": 60 dots fill, 60 × $425 = $25,496, a bar split into car and interest | Shows the time it takes, then splits the total into what he got and what it cost |
| 6 | 35–43 s | The twin | "A *second* Leo": split screen, one Leo drives and one fills a savings jar, "same $425 a month, saved first" | Same person and same monthly amount, opposite choice |
| 7 | 43–46 s | Ask the viewer | "How long does he have to *wait?*" | Makes the viewer guess before the reveal |
| 8 | 46–55 s | The reveal | "He saves *first*": the jar fills to 47 months, the car arrives, "$20,000. Done. No more payments, no interest" | Answers with the same visuals as the problem |
| 9 | 55–58 s | Reframe | "Not cheaper. *Sooner.*" | The twist in two steps |
| 10 | 58–67 s | The insight | "It bought *time*": two timelines with a "waiting" gap, "$5,496 for ≈ 47 months sooner ≈ $117 a month" | Turns the cost into a price per month the viewer can judge |
| 11 | 67–72 s | Be fair | "Sometimes *worth it*": the hero uses the car to get to work | Gives the case where the loan makes sense, which builds trust |
| 12 | 72–82 s | Generalise | "Longer = *pricier* time": 3, 5 and 7-year interest bars, "7 years: almost $8,000" | One rule viewers can apply to their own case |
| 13 | 82–88 s | The truth | "A loan doesn't buy you money. It buys *time.*" plus a footnote with the assumptions | A closing line people can repeat |

## Caricature
- One recurring hero with a first name, drawn as a simple pencil sketch.
- Greyscale world with one colour pop on the thing the hero wants (in Accrevise this is amber).
- The hero appears at the decision and again when the story turns (the "second" version of them).
- Illustrations go on the story beats. The maths beats use clean hand-drawn diagrams: dot grids, filling jars, hatched bars and timelines.

## Storytelling
- The arc runs from need to choice to cost, then the twist, then the closing truth.
- Use one worked example, and keep its numbers the same from start to finish.
- Include a twin or "what if" scene: the same money with a different choice.
- Ask the viewer one question before the reveal.
- Reframe instead of preaching, and include a fair "sometimes it's worth it" beat.
- End on a line that is easy to quote, plus a footnote that states the assumptions.

## On-screen grammar
- The headline sits top-left in a serif, 2–5 words, with the key word in coloured italic.
- Each scene builds in 2–3 steps, about 6–7 s per beat.
- Every number has a unit, and loss figures use the loss colour.

## Fitting it to Accrevise
- Start with the white reel cover. Beat 1 then opens on a negative hook over the hero, for example "This loan quietly costs Rohan ₹1.3 lakh."
- The hero should be an Indian everyday person: a salaried 28-year-old, a parent or a small-shop owner. Use ₹ figures and check every rate.
- Colours: paper ground with green-900 ink for story and maths. The twist and the closing truth go on ledger green. Loss figures use the loss red and the fix uses amber.
- Close with the closing line, a share CTA ("send this to someone taking a loan") and then the kite → envelope → logo outro.

## Checklist
- [ ] A named character appears in the first 3 seconds
- [ ] One worked example, with numbers that never change
- [ ] A twin or "what if" scene
- [ ] One question to the viewer before the reveal
- [ ] A fair "sometimes it's worth it" beat
- [ ] Ends with a closing line, footnote, CTA and outro
