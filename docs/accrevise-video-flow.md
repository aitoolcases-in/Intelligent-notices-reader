# Accrevise video flow: character and storytelling

Every Accrevise video uses this 13-beat story structure, in Accrevise's own design system.
**Never reuse another creator's name or handle, series tag (e.g. "in 90 seconds"), character, wording or exact look.** We keep only the general storytelling structure; the hero, copy, visuals and branding are ours.

## The 13 beats (example: "Minimum due, explained")

| # | Time | Kicker | Beat | Example scene | What it does |
|---|------|--------|------|---------------|--------------|
| 1 | 0–5 s | ACCREVISE EXPLAINS | Hook + promise | "Minimum due, ==explained.==" with ₹50,000 · 3.5% a month · 5% minimum, over Rohan holding his new phone | Topic and the example's numbers up front |
| 2 | 5–12 s | MEET ROHAN | Meet the hero | "Rohan buys a ==phone==": the phone with a ₹50,000 tag, "he pays only the minimum: ₹2,500" | A name, a need and a decision |
| 3 | 12–19 s | WHAT IT IS | Define it | "The minimum ==due==": a card-statement receipt, ticks one by one | Three plain features, revealed one at a time |
| 4 | 19–25 s | THE NUMBERS | The offer | "Rohan's ==bill==": owes / interest / pays boxes, footnote | Fix the numbers used for the rest of the video |
| 5 | 25–34 s | THE REAL COST | The real cost | "One year ==later==": 12 dots fill, ₹28,286 paid, still owes ₹43,910, a bar split into debt cut vs interest | Shows the time, then what the money really did |
| 6 | 34–40 s | WHAT IF | The twin | "A ==second== Rohan": split screen, one pays the minimum, one pays ₹2,500 flat | Same person, same money, opposite choice |
| 7 | 40–44 s | YOUR GUESS | Ask the viewer | On ledger green: "How long until he's ==debt-free?==" | Makes the viewer guess before the reveal |
| 8 | 44–52 s | THE ANSWER | The reveal | "He keeps it ==fixed==": the debt column empties in 39 months | Answers with the same visuals as the problem |
| 9 | 52–57 s | THE TWIST | Reframe | On ledger green: "Minimum due ≠ ==minimum cost.==" | The twist in two steps |
| 10 | 57–66 s | WHY IT HAPPENS | The insight | "It shrinks ==with you==": the minimum falls from ₹2,500 to ₹200; ~23 yrs vs 3¼ yrs | Turns the cost into something the viewer can judge |
| 11 | 66–72 s | TO BE FAIR | Be fair | "Sometimes ==worth it==": a one-off tight month | The honest case, which builds trust |
| 12 | 72–80 s | THE RULE | Generalise | "Pay more, ==pay less==": interest bars for minimum / ₹2,500 / ₹5,000 / ₹10,000 | One rule viewers can apply to their own case |
| 13 | 80–87 s | (large logo) | The truth | On ledger green: "Minimum due. Maximum ==interest.==" plus the assumptions footnote | A closing line people can repeat |

==word== = the amber marker highlight.

## Caricature
- One recurring, named Indian hero, drawn as simple line art in Accrevise green with green hatching.
- One amber colour pop on the thing the hero wants.
- The hero appears at the decision and again when the story turns (the "second" version of them).
- Illustrations go on the story beats. Maths beats use clean drawn diagrams: dot grids, filling columns, hatched bars, receipts and timelines.

## Storytelling
- The arc runs from need to choice to cost, then the twist, then the closing truth.
- Use one worked example, and keep its numbers the same from start to finish.
- Include a twin or "what if" scene: the same money with a different choice.
- Ask the viewer one question before the reveal.
- Reframe instead of preaching, and include a fair "sometimes it's worth it" beat.
- End on a line that is easy to quote, plus a footnote that states the assumptions.

## Accrevise look
- Accrevise logo on every frame (top-left; large and centred on the last frame).
- Paper ground; ledger green for the question, the twist and the closing truth.
- UPPERCASE kicker above a Montserrat 800 headline with one word in the amber marker; Source Serif 4 body.
- The brand ring behind each frame, receipts for bills, the green wipe between beats.
- About 6–7 s per beat, each built in 2–3 steps; 60–90 s in total. Every number has a unit; loss figures in loss red.
