## Caption
Think UPI money is invisible to tax? It isn't. Here's how to stay safe 👇

✅ There's no tax on UPI itself (the "GST on UPI over ₹2,000" message was fake).
⚠️ But income is taxable however you receive it: cash, UPI or bank transfer.

Every UPI payment lands in your bank account, a permanent trail. The tax department sees a lot through your Annual Information Statement (AIS) and can ask for bank records. Notices usually come when the money in your bank is far higher than the income you declare.

3 habits to stay safe:
1️⃣ A separate bank account for your business
2️⃣ A note on every big credit (a friend repaying dinner isn't income)
3️⃣ Check your AIS on the income tax e-filing portal once a year, before you file

AI can sort a year of bank entries in minutes. For tax decisions, talk to a CA.

UPI isn't the problem. Undeclared income is.

Save this, and send it to a shopkeeper who takes UPI all day 📲

## Facts used (and what we did not claim)
- No GST on UPI payments: Finance Ministry called the "GST on UPI above ₹2,000" claim false and baseless (April 2025).
- Income is taxable regardless of how it is received.
- AIS (on the e-filing portal) shows interest, dividends, high-value transactions and TDS; the department can seek bank records during scrutiny.
- Not claimed: that every UPI payment is automatically reported to the tax department (sources disagree).
- ₹9 lakh vs ₹3 lakh mismatch numbers are illustrative.
- General information, not tax advice.
