# "Prices fell, so you stocked up?" — working capital + financial ratios (Ronnie)

**On-screen hook (beat 1):** Prices fell, so you stocked up? → Here's the hidden cost.

## Caption
Prices fell 10%, so he bought ₹15 lakh of stock. His "saving" shrank to ₹24,000. 😶
Here's what went wrong 👇

Ronnie runs a kirana store. His wholesaler offered oil and dal 10% cheaper.
So he put all his cash + a ₹9 lakh overdraft into stock.

What happened:
🔄 His cash wheel (cash → stock → sales → cash) used to turn every 15 days. Now the extra stock took 4 months to sell.
💸 Overdraft interest: ₹9,000 every month
📦 Expired / damaged stock: ₹40,000
🏃 No cash to restock bread, milk, eggs, so customers went next door

₹1 lakh saved → ₹24,000 left.

This is what financial ratios catch early:
📊 Quick ratio = cash you can use ÷ bills you owe. Around 1 is healthy. Ronnie's fell to 0.1.
📊 Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities. Around 1.5–2 is a common benchmark.
📊 Inventory days = how long stock sits. His went from 15 to 50.

Too low → you can't pay bills. Too high → money sits idle. Both mean trouble.
(Healthy ranges vary by industry; these are common rules of thumb.)
